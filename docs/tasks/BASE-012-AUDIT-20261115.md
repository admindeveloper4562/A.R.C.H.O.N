### Risks and platform constraints

- The architecture asks for five SDA worker slots, but it also requires quota and
  resource-aware bounded concurrency. The observed five-call success should not
  be converted into an unattended five-worker authorization without quota
  evidence and sustained-run review.
- `/home` has only `10,967,793,664` bytes available at probe time. The audit did
  not delete, clean, rotate, or otherwise alter files.
- The architecture specifies no stealth downgrade or quota-circumventing account
  rotation. Missing quota data therefore remains an explicit blocker.
- The architecture approval digest mismatch is a separate provenance blocker;
  the observed file was not edited to force the supplied digest.

## S03 audit / decision record

Recommendation: retain BASE-012 as `NOT_VERIFIED` until an owner-authorized,
non-secret provider usage receipt records the exact quota dimensions relevant to
the account (limit, window, remaining allowance and any concurrency/rate ceiling)
and distinguishes model-specific from account-wide limits. Then repeat the
concurrency test under a documented cap, record the maximum accepted level and
failure response, and bind the result to the provider receipt timestamp.

The current safe operational conclusion is: `gpt-5.6-luna` and `gpt-6-sol` are
available now; five simultaneous calls succeeded once; exact quota and maximum
capacity are unknown.

Rejected alternatives:

- Treating the architecture's requested five slots as proof of provider capacity.
- Treating one successful five-call burst as the exact maximum concurrency.
- Inferring quota or billing limits from token counts, exit status, or model text.
- Reading credential stores, private prompts, full databases, or unrelated OS
  files to discover account limits.
- Silently substituting another model or changing the architecture digest.
- Launching the Astra Master, five-worker pipeline, Docker tests, or deployment
  during this bounded audit.
